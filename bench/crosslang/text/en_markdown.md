# Harbour notes

- **Note**: The harbour office opens at seven in the morning and closes when the last ferry has left the quay.

- **Note**: Most of the work is routine: the clerk checks the tickets, writes the weight of each lorry in the register and calls the crew when a ramp is free.

- **Note**: On a calm day the crossing takes forty minutes, and the same boat makes six return trips before the evening.

- **Note**: When the wind turns to the north the swell enters the channel, so the captain waits for the tide and the passengers wait in the hall with their coffee.

- **Note**: Nobody complains much, because everyone on the island has missed a boat at least once and knows that the sea decides.

- **Note**: In winter the timetable is shorter and the office keeps a list of the people who need to reach the hospital on the mainland.

- **Note**: The list is read aloud every morning, and a place is kept for each of them whatever the weather does.

- **Note**: That small habit is older than the building itself, and the clerks are quietly proud of it.
