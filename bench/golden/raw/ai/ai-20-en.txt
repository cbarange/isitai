My grandfather was a cabinetmaker, and I spent a lot of summers in his workshop as a child. I do not think I understood his trade at all until I tried to cut a straight line myself. He worked slowly, which annoyed me, and he used to say that a rushed gesture always costs more than a slow one. His workshop smelled of sawdust and linseed oil, a smell I would recognise anywhere. He let me sand a single drawer for a whole week, and at the time I thought it was absurd. Now I understand he was teaching me to feel the wood rather than follow an instruction. He refused to varnish anything before it had sat in the shop for several days, so the timber could settle. When he died we found half-finished pieces he had never been willing to complete in a hurry. I think of him whenever I am about to rush something, and it slows me down in the right way.
