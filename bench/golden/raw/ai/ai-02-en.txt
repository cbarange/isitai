Mountain hiking rewards preparation far more than enthusiasm. The first step is to pick a route that matches both your fitness and the season, using topographic maps and recent trail reports rather than guesswork. Elevation gain usually matters more than distance, because a steady climb taxes the legs and the lungs in a way that flat kilometres never do. Basic gear includes boots with a stiff sole and deep tread, a windproof shell, and at least a litre and a half of water per person. Weather at altitude shifts quickly, so a clear morning says very little about the afternoon. That is why a spare warm layer, a headlamp and a small emergency blanket belong in the pack even on a short outing. Walking pace should stay even, with brief pauses each hour instead of one long rest. Finally, tell someone where you are going and stay on marked trails, which protect the terrain as much as they protect you.
