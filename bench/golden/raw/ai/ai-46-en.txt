Aisles in hardware stores are busier than ever, and kitchen storage has taken an unexpected place in them. Airtight boxes, drawer dividers and modular shelving now outsell cooking utensils in some outlets. One store manager says the customer base has shifted over two years, with younger buyers who care about how containers look. The trend is also driven by the volume of images of tidy kitchens circulating on social platforms. Home organising companies have sprung up accordingly, offering single-day visits billed at several hundred euros. Not all of them convince: one customer says she paid for a result she could have reached alone in a weekend. Ergonomists point out that layout matters more than containers, and that over-specified systems unravel quickly. The real difficulty remains decluttering, which rarely fits into a single day. Sales, meanwhile, keep climbing.
