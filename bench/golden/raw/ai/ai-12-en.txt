I came to the mountains almost by accident, on a weekend I had not planned properly. The first pass cost me more than I expected, and I remember stopping every hundred metres wondering what I was doing there. What stayed with me was the silence above the tree line, a silence nothing prepares you for and which makes voices sound strange. Since then I have learned to start early, walk slowly and read the map before committing to a trail I do not know. I no longer bring music, because the sound of stones under my boots is enough to keep my mind occupied. I still underestimate descents, especially late in the day when my legs start to shake. The last time out, a shepherd gave me water and told me to change my route because of a storm coming in. I listened, and I was glad I did. I think the mountains have mostly taught me to accept not reaching the top.
