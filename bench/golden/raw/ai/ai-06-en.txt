Organising a kitchen works best when the layout follows movement rather than category. Prep space should stay clear, because that is where most of the daily work actually happens. Knives, boards and mixing bowls belong within arm's reach between the sink and the hob, so nothing requires crossing the room. Dry goods keep well in clear stackable containers, which prevent duplicate purchases and make low stock obvious at a glance. Spices are best grouped near the hob, on a two-tier rack or in a shallow drawer away from direct light. Appliances used once a month can live on high or low shelves, while the daily coffee machine and toaster stay out. One drawer for everyday utensils prevents scattering and saves a surprising amount of time. A final rule that rarely fails is to empty a space completely before putting anything back into it, otherwise the clutter simply moves rather than resolves.
