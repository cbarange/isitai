The meeting is the most widespread coordination unit in project organisations and yet one of the least theorised. Research on organisational communication has shown that meeting length depends less on the number of items covered than on the absence of explicit closure. Where no mechanism signals the end of an item, discussion tends to extend through successive reformulations. Decision-making is also frequently deferred for want of clarity about who holds authority to settle the matter. Minutes, when they exist, are often written after the fact and thereby lose part of their performative value. Facilitation devices, whether human or tool-based, act mainly by making remaining time visible and by recording decisions at the moment they are taken. Available evaluations report shorter sessions, but effects on decision quality remain contested. Some authors note that time pressure may lead groups to dismiss pertinent objections. The question therefore deserves cautious treatment before any generalisation.
