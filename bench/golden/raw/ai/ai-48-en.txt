Meetings take up a growing share of working time, and companies are starting to count the cost. A survey of three hundred employees at an industrial group found that close to a third of the week is spent in them. Figures vary widely by department, with a threefold gap between technical teams and support functions. Several management teams have tried scrapping the weekly meeting, with limited success, for want of another channel to settle trade-offs. One team leader says she shortened her sessions by announcing a time budget per item at the opening. The method has limits: when a topic is contentious, the timer merely relocates the argument. Software vendors now offer tools that log decisions and generate minutes automatically. Staff representatives are asking how those recordings are traced and how long they are kept. The debate about meeting effectiveness is hardly new, but it now has instruments with which to measure itself.
