The massif's trails take on more walkers every summer, and park rangers are struggling to keep pace. According to counters installed at three trailheads, visitor numbers have risen by nearly half in five years. Parking now spills onto the meadows by eight in the morning, which creates friction with grazing herds. A livestock farmer in the valley says his animals have grown used to the traffic, but that unleashed dogs remain a source of tension. The commune introduced a shuttle from the village this summer, free at weekends, which carried close to two thousand people in two months. The scheme is expected to run again next year, though its funding has not yet been settled. Walking groups, for their part, are asking for clearer signage around pastoral zones. Repairs to the stone steps on the most eroded section are due to begin in autumn. One question nobody quite answers remains: whether to keep promoting mountain walking while telling people to come less often.
