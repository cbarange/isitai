To choose a bicycle that you will actually ride, begin by recording your real journeys for two weeks. Note distance, surface, elevation, load and whether the trip ends somewhere secure enough to leave a bike. Set a budget that includes a lock, lights, mudguards and a service, because these are not optional extras in practice. Decide the tyre width from the surface, favouring the widest that fits your frame and your fenders. Select a frame by reach and stack rather than by the size letter, and check standover height with both feet flat. Confirm the gearing range using the ratio of your smallest chainring to your largest sprocket. Verify that the frame has the mounts you need for racks, mudguards and, if relevant, a front carrier. Test ride at least three candidates on the same route, including the worst surface you expect. Have a professional fit performed before buying accessories, since the fit may change the frame size. Finally, book the first service at the point of purchase and ride the bike home rather than transporting it.
