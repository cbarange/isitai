It is tempting to treat a mechanical watch as obsolete, since any phone keeps better time for nothing. That judgement mistakes the function of the object for its purpose, which is precision expressed through craft rather than precision alone. A quartz movement is more accurate by orders of magnitude, and no honest argument can deny it. Yet accuracy was never the only value a watch carried, even before electronics existed. The mechanical movement is a solution to a hard physical problem, built by people who could not fall back on a vibrating crystal. Owning one is closer to owning a hand-written manuscript than to owning a clock, in that the imperfection is part of the thing itself. Defenders often overreach by claiming that mechanical watches are somehow more reliable, which is simply false and weakens their case. The stronger argument is cultural: an industry of micro-mechanics survived and still trains apprentices because people chose to keep it alive. Servicing costs are real and should be disclosed honestly rather than romanticised. The reasonable position is that a mechanical watch is an object of meaning, and that this is enough justification for a choice which never claimed to be efficient.
