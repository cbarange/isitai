To build a working method, begin with one week of observation rather than with a new tool. Record what you actually do in half-hour blocks, including interruptions, and mark which tasks produced something finished. At the end of the week, identify the two or three activities that generated most of the value and the ones that consumed time without output. Write a single queue of commitments, ordered, with a clear definition of what done means for each item. Set a limit on how many items may be in progress at once, and choose a number you can defend on a bad day. Reserve one uninterrupted block per day for the highest-value activity and defend it in your calendar rather than in your intentions. Establish a weekly review of thirty minutes to close finished items, prune stale ones and choose the next week's priorities. Capture every incoming request in the queue immediately, and answer with a date rather than with silence. Measure for a month whether the number of finished items rose, and adjust only one variable at a time. Keep the method only if it survives a week of abnormal load, and discard anything that exists solely to be maintained.
