Think of income tax as a staircase rather than a single slope, with a different rate on each step. Each rate applies only to the slice of income that falls inside its step, never to the whole amount. Climbing one step therefore never makes your entire income jump to the higher rate, which is the most common misunderstanding in the whole system. Before you reach the stairs at all, certain sums are subtracted from gross income to produce the taxable figure. Some reliefs change the height of a step, while others reduce the tax directly, and the second kind is generally worth more. Your employer sends an advance payment every month, and the annual return then settles the difference between that advance and the real debt. A credit differs from a deduction because it can exceed the tax due and turn into a refund. Investment income often follows a separate schedule with its own thresholds and allowances. Keeping receipts for several years remains wise, since an audit can arrive long after filing. Understanding these few rules prevents the absurd decisions people take at the mere mention of a threshold.
