Personal income tax is computed on a taxable base obtained by subtracting allowable deductions from gross income. That base is then divided into brackets, each bracket applying a marginal rate only to the portion of income falling within it. The effective rate, equal to total tax divided by gross income, is therefore always lower than the marginal rate of the highest bracket reached. Standard deductions are fixed amounts granted without justification, while itemised deductions require documentary evidence and are capped by category. Withholding at source is an advance payment reconciled against the final liability at the annual filing. Credits differ from deductions in that they reduce the tax itself rather than the base, which makes them structurally more valuable per unit. Capital gains are frequently taxed under a separate schedule with its own threshold and holding-period conditions. Penalties accrue on underpayment from the statutory due date, independently of any extension to file. Record retention obligations typically span three to seven years depending on jurisdiction and on whether the return was audited. An assessment becomes final once the statutory review window closes, unless a material omission is established.
