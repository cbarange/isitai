To regulate a mechanical watch, begin by letting it run for a full day so that the mainspring settles into its normal torque range. Set it precisely against a reliable time reference and record the deviation at the same hour each day. Wear it normally for a week, since the rate in a drawer differs from the rate on a wrist. Compute the average daily gain or loss and ignore single-day outliers caused by unusual activity. Open the case back only with the correct tool, and place the movement in a dust-free space with good light. Locate the regulator index and note its position photographically before making any adjustment. Move the index by a small increment towards the minus side to slow the watch, and towards the plus side to speed it up. Reassemble, wear it again for several days, and measure the new rate under identical conditions. Repeat in smaller steps, because the relationship is not perfectly linear and overshooting is easy. Stop adjusting once the rate is within your tolerance, and record the final position for the next service.
