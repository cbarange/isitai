Working efficiently looks less like a personality trait than like a handful of ordinary habits. The first is to write down what you have to do instead of keeping it in your head. An external list frees attention and shows you, at a glance, what is committed and what is not. The second is to limit how many things you start at once. Three open projects tire you more than six finished ones, because each one demands an expensive reload of context. The third is to cut work into pieces small enough to finish in one sitting, which gives you clean stopping points. The fourth is to protect blocks of uninterrupted time, because deep work needs about twenty minutes just to begin. The fifth is to separate what is urgent from what is important, urgency usually being a symptom of missing anticipation. The sixth is to review the whole thing regularly, once a week is enough, and delete what no longer makes sense. That last point is the one people skip and the one that pays most, because a method is only worth something if it survives a busy week.
