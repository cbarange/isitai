The envelope arrived in February, as it did every year, and Maria left it on the kitchen table for three days. When she finally opened it, the figure at the bottom was larger than she had expected by a margin that felt like a mistake. She had started a small consultancy in the spring, invoicing four clients and keeping receipts in a shoebox. The withholding on her old salary had stopped, and nothing had replaced it, which the form stated plainly and without sympathy. Her first instinct was to call the number on the letter, and her second was to call her brother, who had been self-employed for a decade. He listened, laughed once, and told her the shoebox was the actual problem. They spent a Sunday afternoon separating the receipts into categories, and the total of deductible expenses turned out to be far higher than she had imagined. The software she had avoided for a year produced a revised estimate in eleven minutes. She paid the balance, set aside a monthly amount, and photographed the shoebox before throwing it away. The lesson she kept was not about tax but about records, and she never opened an envelope with dread again.
