Choosing a bicycle starts with one question that people usually skip: where will you actually ride it. A daily six-kilometre commute on asphalt asks for a very different machine from a three-hour loop on gravel tracks. The frame sets your body position, and that position decides comfort long before any list of components does. A frame that is too long stretches your arms, one that is too short folds your knees, and both make fatigue arrive sooner. Tyres are the second decisive choice, because width and suppleness change efficiency and grip more than any derailleur. Hydraulic disc brakes stop better in the rain, at the cost of slightly more technical maintenance. Modern drivetrains offer very wide ranges, and a double chainring covers most real-world use. Money is better spent on good wheels and tyres than on a top groupset bolted to mediocre wheels. A professional fit, saddle and cleats included, prevents a good share of knee and back pain. Finally, the bike you use is the one you are willing to take out in the rain, which argues for mudguards and fixed lights.
