Makes 1 medium loaf

Ingredients:
100 g (about ½ cup) active sourdough starter, bubbly and recently fed
350 g (1½ cups) lukewarm water
500 g (about 4 cups) bread flour, plus a little extra for shaping
10 g (1¾ teaspoons) fine salt
Rice flour or parchment paper, for lining the proofing bowl or basket

1. Check that your starter is active: after feeding, it should look bubbly and have risen noticeably. In a large mixing bowl, stir the starter into the water until mostly dissolved. Add the bread flour and mix with a sturdy spoon or your hand until no dry patches remain. The dough will be rough and sticky. Cover the bowl and let it rest for 30 minutes.

2. Sprinkle the salt over the dough. Wet one hand and pinch and fold the dough repeatedly to incorporate the salt, turning the bowl as you work. Continue for about 2 minutes, until the dough feels more cohesive. Cover and let it rest for 20 minutes.

3. Knead by hand in the bowl or on a lightly damp, clean counter. To knead in the bowl, grab one edge of the dough, stretch it upward, and fold it over the center; turn the bowl and repeat. For counter kneading, gently stretch the dough and fold it back over itself rather than pressing in extra flour. Knead for 5–8 minutes, until it is smoother and elastic, though it may remain slightly tacky. If it sticks, dampen your hands instead of adding much flour.

4. Put the dough back in the bowl and cover it. Let it rise at room temperature for about 4–6 hours, depending on the warmth of your kitchen and the strength of your starter. During the first 2 hours, give it a set of folds every 30 minutes: lift one side of the dough, stretch it gently, and fold it across the top. Rotate the bowl and repeat on all four sides. Cover between sets. The dough is ready for shaping when it looks puffier, has bubbles at the edges, and has increased in volume by roughly one-third to one-half. It need not double.

5. Lightly flour the counter and turn out the dough. Fold the edges toward the center to make a loose round, then flip it seam-side down. Let it rest uncovered for 15–20 minutes. To shape, flip it seam-side up and gently stretch it into a rough square. Fold the top third down and the bottom third up over it, like folding a letter. Fold the sides toward the center, then roll the dough so the seam is underneath. Cup your hands around it and draw it gently toward you across the counter to create surface tension. Avoid tearing the outer skin.

6. Line a medium bowl or proofing basket with a clean, lightly floured towel; rice flour helps prevent sticking. Place the loaf seam-side up in the bowl. Cover it and let it rise for 1–2 hours at room temperature, or refrigerate it, covered, for 8–14 hours for a slower rise and fuller flavor. It is ready to bake when a gentle fingertip press springs back slowly and leaves a slight impression. If it springs back immediately, give it more time.

7. About 45 minutes before baking, heat the oven to 475°F (245°C) with a lidded Dutch oven inside. If you do not have one, use a heavy oven-safe pot with a lid. Carefully remove the hot pot. Turn the loaf onto a piece of parchment, seam-side down, and use a sharp knife or bread lame to make one slash about ½ inch deep across the top. Lift the parchment and loaf into the pot, cover, and return it to the oven.

8. Bake covered for 20 minutes. Remove the lid, reduce the temperature to 450°F (230°C), and bake another 20–25 minutes, until the crust is deep golden brown. Transfer the loaf to a rack and let it cool for at least 1 hour before slicing. This helps the crumb finish setting. Store cut-side down on a board for a day, then wrap or place in a bag for up to 3 days.
