A new overnight train service linking Paris and Berlin began operations this week, restoring a direct night-time rail connection between the two capitals after years without one. The first train left Paris Gare de l’Est on Monday evening and arrived at Berlin Hauptbahnhof the following morning, according to the operators.

The service is run by Austrian Federal Railways’ Nightjet brand in cooperation with France’s SNCF and Germany’s Deutsche Bahn. It initially operates three times a week, departing Paris and Berlin in the evening and arriving the next morning. The journey takes about 13 hours and includes sleeping cars, couchettes and reclining seats.

The operators said the route offers passengers an alternative to short-haul flights and complements daytime high-speed services between France and Germany. The train stops at several cities en route, including Strasbourg, Karlsruhe, Frankfurt and Erfurt, subject to the timetable.

The launch is part of a broader European push to expand night rail travel as travellers and governments seek lower-carbon options for medium-distance journeys. Rail companies have warned that night trains require coordinated investment in rolling stock, track access and cross-border ticketing to remain viable. The Paris-Berlin service is expected to be reviewed as demand develops.
