Thousands of demonstrators took to the streets in cities across the country on Tuesday to protest government plans to raise the retirement age, in a renewed show of opposition to a pension overhaul that has strained relations between unions and the executive. Marches were organised by major labour confederations, which said participation exceeded their expectations, while authorities reported lower but still substantial crowds. In the capital, protesters gathered near the finance ministry before moving toward the city centre, carrying banners demanding that the government withdraw the reform and open fresh negotiations. Police deployed in strength, and isolated scuffles broke out at the margins of the procession; officials said several people were detained. Union leaders warned that further strikes would follow unless ministers agreed to concessions on contribution periods and early retirement for workers in physically demanding jobs. The government defended the changes as necessary to preserve the viability of the pension system as the population ages, and said it remained open to dialogue while ruling out abandoning the core measure. Public transport, schools and some public services faced disruption, though authorities said overall disruption was limited compared with earlier rounds of protest. The standoff is expected to intensify in the coming weeks as unions prepare a new day of action and parliament resumes debate on the legislation.
