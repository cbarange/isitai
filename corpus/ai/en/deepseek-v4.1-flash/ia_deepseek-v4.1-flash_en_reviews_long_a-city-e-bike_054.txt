I’ve been riding this city e-bike for a little over four months now, mostly for a 4.5-mile commute, grocery runs, and the occasional weekend coffee ride, and I wanted to leave a review that’s actually useful instead of just first-week excitement. Short version: it has genuinely replaced my car for most short trips, and I’m really glad I bought it. Longer version: there are a few quirks I wish I’d known about, but none of them are dealbreakers for me.

The thing that surprised me most is how quickly it became my default way to get around. I live in a city with a few decent hills, and I’m not a hardcore cyclist — I just wanted something that made errands less annoying and commuting less sweaty. This bike does that. The pedal assist feels natural enough once you get used to it, and I usually ride in the middle setting. On my commute I average around 16–18 mph without feeling like I’m rushing, and I still get a little exercise. The range has been solid for my use: I’m getting roughly 30–35 miles per charge in mixed terrain, less if I leave it in the highest assist or ride into a strong headwind. In colder weather I noticed a drop, which I expected, but it hasn’t stranded me.

Assembly was easier than I feared. It arrived mostly put together, and I only had to attach the front wheel, handlebars, and pedals. The brakes needed a small adjustment out of the box — they were a little rubby — and I ended up taking it to a local shop for a quick safety check. I’d recommend doing that if you’re not comfortable with bike maintenance. After that, it’s been smooth. The gears are simple and reliable, and the drivetrain has stayed quiet with basic chain cleaning.

Comfort is a big plus for a city bike. The upright riding position feels natural in traffic, and the step-through frame makes stopping at lights and hopping off with groceries much easier. The saddle is decent — not amazing, but fine for rides under an hour. The grips are comfortable, and the fenders actually keep road spray off my shoes. The rear rack is sturdy enough for panniers, and I’ve used it for a full grocery run more than once. The lights are bright and wired to the battery, which is one of those features I didn’t think much about until I started riding home after dark. I do wish the headlight had a slightly wider beam on unlit side streets, and there are no turn signals, so I still use hand signals like on any bike.

Now the honest cons. It’s heavy — around 55 pounds — and carrying it up a flight of stairs is a chore. If you live in a walk-up, think carefully about storage. The assist is cadence-based rather than torque-based, so there’s a slight delay when you start pedaling, and it can feel a bit surgey in the lowest gear. It’s not a dealbreaker, but it’s not the most refined e-bike feel out there. The display is basic and there’s no app integration, which is fine by me but might bother someone who likes tracking everything. Also, the kickstand is stable when the bike is empty but feels a little flexy when I’m loading groceries on the rear rack.

Build quality has been good for the price. The frame feels solid, the paint has held up, and I haven’t had any electrical issues. Customer service was responsive when I asked about a replacement key for the battery — slow-ish, but helpful. I’d recommend checking the battery lock and tire pressure every couple of weeks, and definitely get a good lock, because this bike is not light enough to casually carry inside everywhere.

Would I buy it again? Yes. It’s not a sporty e-bike and it won’t impress someone who wants a torque sensor and a sleek integrated battery, but as a practical city bike for commuting, errands, and casually getting around, it’s been excellent. It’s made me drive less, and that alone has been worth it. If you want a comfortable, useful, no-drama city e-bike and you have a safe place to store something on the heavier side, I’d recommend it.
