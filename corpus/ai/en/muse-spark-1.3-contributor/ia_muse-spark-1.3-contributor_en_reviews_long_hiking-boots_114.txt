My feet, my knees, and 200 miles later - honest thoughts after 4 months

I'm 43, not an ultralight thru-hiker or a gear nerd, just a guy who tries to get out every weekend with my dog and my 14-year-old son. I have flat-ish wide feet, a right knee that clicks every time I go downhill since an old soccer injury, and a history of buying hiking boots that feel great in the store and then destroy me on mile six. I bought the KEEN Targhee III Waterproof Mid in Bison/Mulch, size 10.5 Wide, back in late June after my old Columbias literally fell apart on the Old Rag loop in Shenandoah - sole peeling off, laces soaked, blisters on both heels. I was miserable, limping the last three miles, and I swore I was done cheaping out on footwear.

I spent almost two weeks reading reviews before I pulled the trigger here. I chose these because I needed something sturdy but not super stiff and heavy like full leather backpacking boots, waterproof but breathable for summer in Virginia, and with real ankle support without feeling like ski boots. I also have a wide forefoot and most Salomons and Merrells pinch my toes, while KEENs have that reputation for a roomier toe box. I paid full price, around $180, which hurt a little, but I told my wife it was cheaper than physical therapy.

Sizing and first fit: I ordered my normal sneaker size, 10.5, but in Wide, and that was exactly right for me. Right out of the box they felt... substantial. Not heavy-clunky, but solid. About 1 lb 6 oz per boot, you feel them but they don't drag. The toe box was gloriously roomy - I could wiggle my toes even with my thicker Darn Tough hiking socks on. No pinching on my bunion on the left foot, which is rare. The heel cup felt snug and a little stiff at first, and the arch support was higher than I'm used to. I have low arches, so I worried it would ache, but it turned out to be supportive rather than pushy. My advice: if you're between sizes, don't size up for thick socks, just get your true size and the Wide if you need it. Length runs true.

Break-in was surprisingly painless. I wore them around the house for an evening, then did a short 3-mile flat loop by the river the next day. Zero hot spots, which never happens for me. Second outing was a 7-mile hilly hike at Prince William Forest with some mud and creek crossings. My son was breaking in new trail runners at the same time and got blisters - I had nothing. Not even a red spot. By the third hike I stopped thinking about my feet entirely, which is honestly the highest praise I can give a boot.

The real test came in August when we did a three-day, two-night trip in Dolly Sods, West Virginia, about 26 miles total with a 28-pound pack. Lots of rocks, boggy meadows, roots, sudden downpours, and those long rocky descents that usually kill my knee. Two things stood out. First, the support and cushioning. The midsole is firm enough that I didn't feel every sharp rock through my sole like with my old shoes, but there's enough give that my knees weren't jarred. Going downhill, my ankle felt locked in without my Achilles rubbing. I use the heel-lock lacing on the top hook and it really works. My knee still clicked, but I wasn't in pain at camp, and that has never happened after back-to-back 9-mile days. Second, grip. On wet sandstone and slick muddy boardwalks, these things just stuck. I slipped once on a slimy root, but that would trip a mountain goat. My son slipped four times in his trail runners on the same section.

Waterproofing has been genuinely good, not just marketing. I've walked through ankle-deep puddles, steady rain for four hours in Shenandoah in September, and dewy tall grass every morning walking the dog. My socks stayed dry every time, except once when water came in over the top of the ankle collar - my fault, obviously. They do get warm. In 85-degree humid July weather my feet were sweaty at the end, no way around a waterproof membrane in summer. But they didn't get that swampy, squishy feeling, and they dried overnight. No weird smell yet either, which my wife appreciates, though I do take the insoles out and loosen them to air out.

After roughly 200 miles, how are they holding up? Pretty well.
