Passengers crowded onto Platform 7 at Paris Gare de l'Est on Monday evening as the first scheduled commercial service of the new direct night train to Berlin departed on time at 19:12, restoring an overnight link between the French and German capitals that was discontinued nearly a decade ago and hailed by ministers from both countries as a milestone for European rail transport.

The service, jointly operated by French national operator SNCF Voyageurs, Germany's Deutsche Bahn and Austria's OBB under the Nightjet brand, will initially run three times per week in each direction, departing Paris on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays and returning from Berlin Hauptbahnhof on Tuesdays, Thursdays and Sundays, with the operators promising a daily service by late 2027 if demand meets forecasts.

The 1,050-kilometre journey is scheduled to take just over thirteen hours, arriving in Berlin at 08:26 after stops at Strasbourg, Karlsruhe, Mannheim and Frankfurt Sud, while the southbound train leaves Berlin at 18:53 and reaches Paris at 08:11, allowing business travellers to work a full day before departure and arrive in the city centre in time for morning meetings without the need for airport transfers or hotel nights.

The train comprises seven modernised coaches, including seated carriages with reclining seats, six-berth couchette compartments and private one-, two- and four-berth sleeper cabins with washbasins, bedding and breakfast included, as well as a multifunctional car for bicycles, bulky luggage and passengers with reduced mobility, all fitted with air conditioning, free Wi-Fi, USB sockets and upgraded sound insulation.

Tickets start at 29.90 euros for a reclining seat, 59.90 euros for a couchette and 99.90 euros for a berth in a private sleeper compartment, with family fares, Interrail supplements from 14 euros and full accessibility for holders of France's Carte Avantage and Germany's BahnCard 25 and 50, a pricing strategy the operators say is designed to compete directly with low-cost airlines on the same route.

Transport ministers from France and Germany joined European Commission officials and railway executives on the platform in Paris for a brief inauguration ceremony, cutting a blue ribbon across the locomotive and describing the project as concrete proof of the European Green Deal in action, coming less than a year after the European Union designated 2026 as the Year of Cross-Border Rail and pledged 2.1 billion euros to revive night services across the continent.

According to figures cited by Deutsche Bahn, a journey by night train between the two capitals emits on average 30 times less carbon dioxide per passenger than a short-haul flight and four times less than a car journey with a single occupant, while independent surveys show that nearly two-thirds of French and German travellers under 35 say they are willing to accept longer travel times if a low-emission alternative is comfortable and affordable.

Early booking data suggests strong demand, with OBB reporting that more than 40,000 tickets were sold within the first three weeks of sales opening in September, including fully booked sleeper cabins for the Christmas and New Year period, and SNCF Connect said searches for Paris-Berlin night increased by 380 percent week-on-week after the timetable was published, prompting calls from passenger associations for additional capacity during school holidays.

Passengers interviewed on board the inaugural service expressed enthusiasm tempered by concerns about punctuality and comfort, with 24-year-old Berlin student Lena Hoffmann, travelling with two friends in a couchette, saying she had chosen the train to avoid airport stress and reduce her carbon footprint, while 58-year-old Parisian consultant Marc Lefevre, in a private sleeper, said he hoped the service would prove reliable enough to replace his monthly flight for work.

Railway managers acknowledge significant obstacles remain, from incompatible signalling systems and overnight engineering works to high track-access charges in France and a shortage of night-train rolling stock across Europe, but both governments confirmed that a Paris-Berlin-Brussels extension, a new Zurich-Paris night service and refurbished sleeping cars with showers and private toilets from 2028 are already under study as part of a ten-year plan to double overnight passenger numbers.
