Background: Accurate electricity demand forecasting underpins grid reliability, generation scheduling, and market operation, yet nonlinear weather and calendar effects complicate prediction. Methods: We evaluated gradient-boosted trees, long short-term memory networks, and a hybrid model against seasonal autoregressive baselines using hourly load and meteorological data from a regional grid. Results: The hybrid model reduced mean absolute percentage error by 18% relative to the baseline, with the largest gains during peak-demand periods. Conclusion: Machine learning, particularly hybrid architectures, meaningfully improves short-term load forecasts and merits operational deployment.
