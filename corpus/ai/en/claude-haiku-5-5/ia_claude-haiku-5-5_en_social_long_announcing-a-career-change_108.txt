Okay, deep breath… I'm doing it. 😅 I'm officially changing careers!

If you've been around me for the past few years, you've probably heard me complain about my job at least once a week (sorry, I know I was a lot 🙈). But I also kept quietly thinking about something else, something I kept pushing aside because it felt "too late," "too risky," or "not realistic." Well, I finally got tired of putting it off. So here we are!

For the last ten years, I worked in [your previous field]. I learned a ton there. I met amazing people, I got good at things I didn't even know I could do, and I'm genuinely grateful for all of it. But somewhere along the way, I stopped feeling excited on Monday mornings. I'd open my laptop and feel… nothing. Just a vague tiredness, like I was running on autopilot. And honestly? That scared me more than leaving ever could.

So a few months ago, I started asking myself some uncomfortable questions. What do I actually enjoy doing, even when nobody's watching? When do I lose track of time? What would I regret not trying when I'm 70? 🤔 The answers were kind of obvious once I stopped overthinking. I love [new field/passion]. I love learning it, creating with it, and talking about it with people who get as excited as I do. It's been sitting in the back of my head forever, and it kept showing up every time I felt most alive.

Of course, my brain immediately threw a full panic party 🎉😭. "You're too old to start over." "What if you fail?" "What about the money?" "What will people think?" Yeah, I heard all of it. But I also realized that staying exactly where I was felt like a slow kind of failure. And I'd rather try and fall flat than wake up one day wondering "what if."

So I took some small steps first, the ones that didn't require quitting everything overnight. I took evening classes. I started a little side project just to see if I actually liked the work or if I was just romanticizing it (spoiler: I really like it 😄). I talked to people who already work in this field and asked them a million questions. Some of them were super generous with their time, and that meant more than they probably know.

Now I've reached the point where I'm ready to make the move for real. My last day at my old job is [date], and starting [month], I'll be working as [new job title / doing freelance work in new field]. I'm nervous, and I'm also weirdly calm, which I think means I'm on the right track. 💛

I want to be honest: this isn't a "I found my dream life in three days" kind of story. It's messy. There will be days where I doubt everything, where my new skills feel tiny, where I'm the one asking the obvious question in meetings. But I'd rather be a beginner doing something I care about than an expert feeling empty.

To anyone reading this who's sitting on a similar idea right now: you don't have to have it all figured out. You just have to take one small step toward it. Sign up for the course. Send the message. Make the ugly first draft. You can always adjust as you go. I wish someone had told me that years ago, so I'm telling you now. 🙌

Thank you to everyone who supported me, who listened to me ramble about this for months, and who didn't roll their eyes too hard when I kept saying "maybe next year." You know who you are, and I love you for it. ❤️
