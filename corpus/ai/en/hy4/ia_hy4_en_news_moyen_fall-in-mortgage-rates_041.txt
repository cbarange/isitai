Mortgage rates fell further this week as lenders cut the cost of fixed-rate home loans, offering relief to buyers and households preparing to refinance after two years of sharp increases. Industry data showed average rates on two- and five-year fixes drifting lower, with several high-street banks and building societies trimming standard deals and launching cheaper products for borrowers with larger deposits.

The declines follow a fall in swap rates, the market measures used to price fixed mortgages, and reflect investor expectations that the Bank of England is more likely to cut interest rates than raise them. Lenders have also been competing for new business after a subdued period for housing transactions, when affordability constraints and higher borrowing costs kept many first-time buyers out of the market.

Brokers said the reductions had prompted a rise in enquiries and mortgage applications, particularly from purchasers who had delayed moves while waiting for rates to settle. Some homeowners nearing the end of fixed terms have begun reserving new deals, allowing them to secure a rate months before their existing mortgage expires.

Economists cautioned that the fall does not mark a return to the ultra-low borrowing costs of the decade before inflation surged. Rates remain well above the levels many households locked in before 2022, meaning monthly payments are still likely to rise substantially for those refinancing. House prices have also stayed resilient in parts of the country, limiting the benefit of cheaper credit.

Further declines are expected to depend on wage growth, services inflation and government borrowing costs. The Bank of England has said any reduction in Bank Rate will be judged against evidence that inflation pressures are easing sustainably.
