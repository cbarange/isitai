This rich and velvety soup combines the natural sweetness of roasted butternut squash with the creamy, tropical notes of coconut milk. Enhanced with warming spices like fresh ginger, garlic, and a hint of curry, it makes for a comforting meal on chilly evenings. Roasting the squash before simmering concentrates its sugars and creates a deeper, caramelized flavor profile that elevates the entire dish.

**Preparation and Cooking Time**
* Prep time: 20 minutes
* Cook time: 45 minutes
* Total time: 1 hour 5 minutes
* Servings: 4 to 6

**Ingredients**

*For Roasting:*
* 1 large butternut squash (approximately 3 pounds / 1.3 kg), peeled, seeded, and cut into 1-inch cubes
* 2 tablespoons olive oil or melted coconut oil
* 1 teaspoon fine sea salt
* 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper

*For the Soup Base:*
* 1 tablespoon coconut oil or olive oil
* 1 medium yellow onion, finely diced
* 3 cloves garlic, minced
* 1 tablespoon fresh ginger, peeled and finely grated
* 1 teaspoon mild curry powder
* 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
* 1/4 teaspoon ground coriander
* 1/8 teaspoon cayenne pepper (optional, for heat)
* 3 cups (750 ml) vegetable broth, low sodium
* 1 can (13.5 ounces / 400 ml) full-fat coconut milk, shaken well (reserve 2 tablespoons for garnish)
* 1 tablespoon pure maple syrup or honey (optional, depending on the sweetness of the squash)
* 1 tablespoon fresh lime juice

*For the Garnish:*
* 1/4 cup raw pumpkin seeds (pepitas), toasted
* 1/4 cup fresh cilantro leaves, roughly chopped
* Red pepper flakes or chili oil (optional)

**Instructions**

**Step 1: Roast the Butternut Squash**
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment paper. Place the cubed butternut squash onto the baking sheet. Drizzle with the 2 tablespoons of olive oil, and sprinkle evenly with the sea salt and black pepper. Toss with your hands until every piece is well coated, then spread the cubes out into a single, even layer. Avoid overcrowding the pan, as this will cause the squash to steam rather than caramelize. Roast in the preheated oven for 30 to 35 minutes, flipping the cubes halfway through, until the squash is fork-tender and the edges are lightly browned. Remove from the oven and set aside.

**Step 2: Sauté the Aromatics**
While the squash is finishing in the oven, place a large Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed soup pot over medium heat. Add the 1 tablespoon of coconut oil. Once melted and shimmering, add the diced yellow onion along with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 6 to 8 minutes until the onion is soft, translucent, and just beginning to brown around the edges. 

**Step 3: Toast the Spices**
Add the minced garlic and grated fresh ginger to the pot with the onions. Stir constantly for about 1 minute until become very fragrant, taking care not to let the garlic burn. Add the curry powder, ground cumin, ground coriander, and cayenne pepper. Continue to stir for another 30 to 45 seconds. Toasting the dry spices in the oil awakens their essential oils and significantly deepens the flavor of the soup.

**Step 4: Simmer the Soup**
Pour in about half a cup of the vegetable broth to deglaze the pot, using a wooden spoon to scrape up any browned bits stuck to the bottom. Add the remaining vegetable broth, the roasted butternut squash cubes, and the full-fat coconut milk (making sure to set aside a couple of tablespoons for serving). Stir everything together and increase the heat to medium-high to bring the liquid to a gentle boil. Once boiling, immediately reduce the heat to low, cover the pot partially with a lid, and let it simmer for 10 minutes to allow the flavors to meld together.

**Step 5: Blend Until Smooth**
Remove the pot from the heat. Use an immersion blender (stick blender) directly in the pot to puree the soup until it is completely smooth and velvety. If you do not have an immersion blender, carefully transfer the soup in batches to a standard countertop blender. Never fill a countertop blender more than halfway with hot liquid; remove the center cap from the blender lid and cover the hole with a folded kitchen towel to allow steam to escape safely while blending. Return the pureed soup to the pot.

**Step 6: Adjust Seasoning and Serve**
Stir in the fresh lime juice and the maple syrup, if using. The acidity of the lime brightens the heavy coconut and sweet squash flavors. Taste the soup and add more salt, black pepper, or lime juice as needed. If the soup is thicker than you prefer, stir in an extra splash of warm vegetable broth or water until it reaches your desired consistency.

Ladle the hot soup into individual warmed bowls. Drizzle the reserved coconut milk in a swirl pattern on top of each bowl. Finish by scattering the toasted pumpkin seeds, chopped fresh cilantro, and a pinch of red pepper flakes over the top. Serve immediately alongside crusty bread or warm naan. This soup can be stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 5 days, or frozen for up to 3 months.
