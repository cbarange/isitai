The café on the corner of Elm Street had changed names three times in twenty years, but the scent of roasted coffee and cinnamon still lingered, stubborn as a memory. Elena sat by the window, her fingers tracing the rim of a porcelain cup, watching the door with a quiet, trembling anticipation. She had rehearsed this moment in the silence of countless nights, yet now that it had arrived, every carefully chosen word seemed to dissolve on her tongue.

When Mara finally stepped inside, time seemed to fold in on itself. Her hair, once a wild cascade of auburn, was now threaded with silver and pulled into a neat twist. But her eyes—those same searching, defiant eyes—found Elena’s instantly, and the years between them felt both vast and insignificant.

Neither spoke at first. They simply stood there, two women shaped by two decades of separate joys and sorrows, tethered by the ghost of the girls they used to be. Then Mara smiled, a small, fragile thing, and Elena felt something tighten and release in her chest.

"You came," Elena whispered.

"I almost didn’t," Mara admitted, sliding into the chair opposite her. "But I kept thinking about the cherry tree in Mom’s backyard. How we used to promise we’d never let anything come between us."

Elena laughed softly, tears blurring her vision. "We were terrible at keeping promises."

"Maybe," Mara said, reaching across the table to take her sister’s hand. Her grip was warm, familiar. "But we’re here now."

Outside, the afternoon light spilled across the pavement, golden and forgiving, as if the world itself was offering them a chance to begin again.
