Mortgage rates across the country fell to their lowest level in more than a year this week, offering relief to prospective homebuyers and borrowers looking to refinance, according to data released by major lenders on Thursday.

The average rate on a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage dropped to 6.12 percent, down from 6.45 percent a month ago, marking the steepest monthly decline since mid-2022. Fifteen-year fixed mortgages also fell, averaging 5.38 percent compared with 5.72 percent in the previous month.

Economists attributed the decline largely to market expectations that the central bank will hold interest rates steady through the end of the year, and may begin cutting rates in early 2026. Treasury yields, which closely track mortgage pricing, have slid in recent weeks amid cooling inflation data and weaker-than-expected job figures.

"Affordability is still a major challenge in this market, but this is a meaningful step in the right direction," said Dr. Ellen Marsh, a housing economist at the Urban Policy Institute. "At current price levels, even a modest rate reduction translates to hundreds of dollars in monthly savings for the average borrower."

Housing inventory remains tight in many metropolitan areas, keeping upward pressure on prices despite the cheaper financing. The National Association of Realtors reported that the median existing-home price rose 3.2 percent year over year in the latest quarter.

Refinancing activity has surged accordingly, with applications rising 18 percent over the past two weeks, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association. Lenders anticipate sustained demand through the fourth quarter if rates hold near current levels.

Analysts cautioned, however, that further improvement depends on inflation trends and the labour market, warning that a reversal in economic data could quickly push borrowing costs higher.
