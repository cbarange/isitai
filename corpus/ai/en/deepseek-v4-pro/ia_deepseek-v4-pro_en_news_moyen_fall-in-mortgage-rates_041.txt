Mortgage rates declined this week, offering a measure of relief to prospective homebuyers and owners considering refinancing. The average rate on a 30-year fixed-rate mortgage fell to 6.55 percent from 6.72 percent a week earlier, according to Freddie Mac, reaching its lowest level in three months. The average 15-year fixed rate also moved lower, dropping to 5.84 percent.

The decline tracked a pullback in the yield on the 10-year Treasury note, which lenders use as a benchmark for pricing long-term home loans. Treasury yields eased after recent inflation data came in cooler than expected, reinforcing market expectations that the Federal Reserve could begin cutting its benchmark interest rate later this year.

For borrowers, the change is modest but meaningful. On a $400,000 mortgage, the week-over-week rate reduction lowers the typical monthly principal and interest payment by roughly $45. Refinance applications increased in the latest weekly survey from the Mortgage Bankers Association, though purchase activity remains subdued.

Housing economists said lower borrowing costs may draw some buyers back into the market, but affordability remains constrained by high home prices and limited inventory in many regions. Any sustained decline in rates will help at the margin, analysts said, but the broader supply shortage is not going away.
