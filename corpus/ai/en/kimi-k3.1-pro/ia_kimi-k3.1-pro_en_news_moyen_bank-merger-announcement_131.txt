Harbor National Bank and Meridian Trust announced on Monday that they had agreed to merge in an all-stock transaction valued at $18.4 billion, creating a lender with approximately $420 billion in assets and branches across 22 states.

Under the agreement, Meridian shareholders would receive 1.25 Harbor shares for each Meridian share. Harbor investors would own 58 percent of the combined company, with Meridian shareholders holding the remainder. The banks said the transaction was expected to close in the first quarter of next year, subject to approval by shareholders and federal regulators.

Harbor’s chief executive, Daniel Rowe, would lead the merged institution, while Meridian’s chief executive, Elaine Porter, would serve as chair of its board. The companies said the combined bank would retain Harbor’s name and headquarters in Chicago.

“The merger brings together complementary branch networks and expands our capacity to serve households and businesses,” Rowe said in a statement. The banks projected annual cost savings of $950 million within two years of completion and said investment in digital services would increase.

Customer accounts, loan terms and online banking services would remain unchanged until the transaction is completed, according to the companies. They said overlapping branches would be reviewed but declined to specify how many locations or jobs could be affected. Together, the banks employ about 56,000 people.

The announcement comes as regional lenders face rising technology costs and continued pressure from larger national competitors. Analysts said the merger could improve efficiency but would draw close scrutiny over its potential effects on competition and access to banking services.

A consumer advocacy group urged regulators to examine possible branch closures and lending commitments in lower-income communities before approving the deal.
