The month of October has broken temperature records across multiple regions, with meteorologists confirming unseasonable warmth that has left seasonal expectations far behind. The UK Met Office reported that mean daily temperatures for the month reached an average of 13.4°C, surpassing the previous October record set in 2011 by 0.8°C. Several days saw maximums exceeding 20°C, a threshold more commonly associated with late August.

Across Europe, similar patterns emerged. The Copernicus Climate Change Service recorded October 2023 as the warmest October since global records began, with surface air temperatures approximately 1.7°C above the 1991–2020 average. Southern France and Iberia experienced prolonged periods of above-30°C heat well into the latter half of the month, prompting authorities in several provinces to extend outdoor working restrictions normally enforced during summer months.

In the United States, the National Weather Service confirmed that over 8,000 daily high-temperature records were tied or broken during the month, with the Midwest and Northeast particularly affected. Parts of New England experienced October temperatures comparable to those typically recorded in June.

Scientists attribute the record-breaking conditions to a combination of persistent high-pressure systems, elevated sea surface temperatures linked to El Niño, and the continuing influence of anthropogenic climate change. Agricultural bodies have raised concerns over disrupted harvest cycles and early dormancy in temperate crops, while energy providers anticipate lower-than-expected demand for heating across northern Europe.
