Silkworm farming, or sericulture, is the practice of raising *Bombyx mori* larvae to harvest silk cocoon threads. Originating in China around 2700 BCE, the craft spread along the Silk Road. Farmers feed the larvae exclusively on mulberry leaves, maintaining precise temperature and humidity levels. After spinning their cocoons, the pupae are killed by heat, and the filaments are unwound and reeled into raw silk. Major producers include China, India, and Brazil, where sericulture remains a significant rural economic activity.
