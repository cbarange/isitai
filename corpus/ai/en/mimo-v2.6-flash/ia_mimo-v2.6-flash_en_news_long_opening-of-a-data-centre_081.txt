The inauguration of a major data centre in the northern French region of Hauts-de-France took place on Thursday, marking what operators describe as one of the largest investments in digital infrastructure the country has seen in several years. The facility, which spans more than 80,000 square metres across two interconnected buildings, has been built with a total investment of approximately 480 million euros and is expected to create more than 600 permanent jobs by 2027, according to the company behind the project.

The site, located on the outskirts of the town of Valenciennes near the Belgian border, is the first in a series of planned facilities by the European data centre operator, which announced its expansion strategy to the European market eighteen months ago. Construction began in early 2023 following a prolonged environmental review process and a public consultation that drew several hundred participants. The company stated that the building's design incorporates advanced water-cooling systems to reduce consumption and that its energy supply will be partially covered by on-site solar installations generating an estimated 12 megawatts at peak capacity.

Local and regional officials attended the opening ceremony, during which the president of the Hauts-de-France regional council described the data centre as "a decisive step in the economic transformation" of an area that has struggled with the decline of heavy industry for more than three decades. The regional authority contributed 35 million euros in financial support and infrastructure upgrades, including the construction of a dedicated high-voltage electrical substation to feed the site. The national government also confirmed that the project qualified for tax incentives under the framework designed to attract strategic industrial investments to France.

The operator, which did not disclose the identity of the initial clients leasing space at the facility, indicated that several major cloud computing and telecommunications companies have already signed preliminary agreements. Full occupancy of the first building is expected by the end of 2026, while the second phase of construction, which would double the site's total capacity, remains subject to further regulatory approvals and demand assessments.

Environmental groups have expressed concerns about the facility's electricity consumption. According to publicly available figures, the data centre's projected annual energy demand at full capacity is roughly 180 megawatts, equivalent to the consumption of a mid-sized French city. In a statement released ahead of the inauguration, a coalition of regional environmental organisations called on the operator to provide detailed transparency on its carbon footprint and urged local authorities to impose binding energy-efficiency targets as a condition of continued operation. The company responded that its systems are designed to operate at a power usage effectiveness ratio of 1.15, a figure it described as "well above industry averages in France," and that it plans to enter into long-term contracts with renewable energy producers to cover the remainder of its demand.

The data centre boom across Western Europe has accelerated significantly in the past two years, driven by surging demand for artificial intelligence services and cloud storage. France has positioned itself as a key destination for such investments, with several projects announced in the Paris region, in the Lyon area, and now in the north of the country. Industry analysts estimate that the European data centre market will exceed 30 billion euros in annual revenue by 2030, up from roughly 18 billion euros in 2024.

The Valenciennes site is expected to become fully operational during the first quarter of 2026. According to the company's roadmap, the total combined capacity of all planned facilities across Europe could exceed 600 megawatts by 2029, though specific timelines for those projects have not yet been made public.
