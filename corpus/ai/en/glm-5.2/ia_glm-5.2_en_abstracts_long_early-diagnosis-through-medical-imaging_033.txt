Background

Lung cancer is the leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, and outcomes are strongly stage dependent, with five-year survival exceeding 70% for stage I disease but falling below 10% once distant metastases develop. Low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) screening reduces lung cancer mortality by 20–26%, yet implementation is hampered by high false-positive rates, inter-reader variability, fragmented follow-up, and escalating radiologist workload. Deep-learning algorithms have achieved encouraging retrospective results for pulmonary nodule classification, but robust evidence from fully external validation, blinded comparison with expert readers, and prospective deployment remains scarce. We therefore developed a deep-learning system for early lung cancer detection on LDCT and evaluated it across retrospective, external, reader-study, and prospective randomized settings.

Methods

The study comprised three linked phases. First, model development used 52,384 baseline LDCT scans (71,032 nodules ≥6 mm; 1,523 cancers confirmed within 24 months) from nine screening programs in the United States, the Netherlands, and South Korea (2008–2020), with deliberate enrichment of biopsy-confirmed cancers in training folds. Malignancy was defined by histopathology; benignity was defined by ≥24 months of radiologic stability. Second, the locked model underwent fully external validation on 11,246 consecutive scans (312 cancers) from four hospitals in three countries not represented in training. Third, a prospective, reader-masked pilot randomized 4,318 screening participants 1:1 at five sites (2022–2024) to AI-assisted versus standard double-reading pathways.

The system used a cascaded architecture: a three-dimensional convolutional network for automated nodule detection and segmentation, followed by a volumetric transformer integrating nodule morphology, perinodular parenchymal context, and, when prior imaging existed, longitudinal growth. Training used five-fold cross-validation, class rebalancing, and heavy augmentation; the operating threshold was prespecified on held-out folds, and external testing proceeded without site-specific tuning. All imaging followed standardized LDCT protocols (CTDIvol ≤3.0 mGy; ≤1.25-mm reconstructions; iterative reconstruction).

The primary endpoint was the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUC) for lung cancer within 24 months in external validation. Secondary endpoints included sensitivity and specificity versus Lung-RADS-guided consensus reading, relative false-positive reduction, stage and size at diagnosis in the pilot, a multi-reader multi-case (MRMC) study in which 14 board-certified radiologists interpreted 400 enriched cases (120 malignant) unaided and with AI assistance, a longitudinal lead-time analysis, calibration, and decision-curve net benefit. AUCs were compared with DeLong tests; 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were bootstrap-derived; MRMC data were analyzed with Dorfman–Berbaum–Metz methods; net reclassification improvement was calculated. Two-sided α was 0.05. Reporting followed STARD guidelines; all sites obtained ethics approval and written informed consent.

Results

In external validation, the model achieved an AUC of 0.941 (95% CI, 0.927–0.952) versus 0.884 (95% CI, 0.869–0.901) for radiologist consensus (DeLong P < 0.001). At the prespecified threshold, sensitivity was 94.9% (296/312; 95% CI, 91.8–97.0) and specificity 81.7% (95% CI, 80.9–82.5), compared with 90.1% and 68.9%, respectively, for readers; this corresponded to a 41.2% relative reduction in false-positive referrals (2,001 versus 3,401 per 11,246 scans). Predicted and observed cancer prevalences agreed closely (2.9% versus 2.8%), and decision-curve analysis demonstrated superior net benefit across threshold probabilities of 5–30%.

In the MRMC study, AI assistance increased mean reader AUC from 0.878 (95% CI, 0.855–0.900) to 0.915 (95% CI, 0.896–0.933; P = 0.001), reduced median interpretation time from 96 to 71 seconds (−26%; P < 0.001), raised inter-reader agreement (κ, 0.58 to 0.73), and improved sensitivity for subcentimeter nodules (61.4% to 78.9%).

In the prospective pilot, 90 cancers were diagnosed (47 in the AI-assisted arm, 43 in the control arm). Stage I disease at diagnosis was more frequent with AI assistance (74.5% [35/47] versus 55.8% [24/43]; P = 0.046), and median tumor diameter was smaller (12.4 versus 16.8 mm; P = 0.01). Unnecessary follow-up imaging per 1,000 screens fell from 231 to 142 (P < 0.001), with no significant difference in interval cancers (2 versus 3). In the longitudinal analysis of 96 incident cancers preceded by a negative annual screen, the model had flagged 38 (39.6%) on the prior examination a median of 13.4 months (interquartile range, 11.8–15.9) before diagnosis, when the nodules were smaller (median 8.7 versus 12.1 mm) and 71% were ultimately stage I. Performance was stable across sex, age, and smoking strata (all interaction P > 0.10) and across solid (AUC 0.948) and subsolid (AUC 0.923) nodules. Failure review of the 16 missed cancers identified endobronchial or juxtapleural location in 11.

Conclusion

Across retrospective development, fully external validation, blinded reader comparison, and a prospective randomized pilot, deep-learning-assisted reading improved early lung cancer detection on LDCT, surpassing expert readers in discrimination and calibration, reducing false-positive referrals by more than 40%, shortening interpretation time, and shifting diagnoses toward earlier-stage, smaller tumors. By anticipating conventional detection by more than a year in a substantial minority of incident cancers, the system addresses the principal bottlenecks of LDCT screening—false positives and reader capacity—and could expand access where radiologist shortages limit program adoption. Limitations include the modest prospective sample, restriction to screening populations, and absence of mortality endpoints. Larger randomized trials with survival follow-up are warranted; pending such evidence, the system is suitable for integration as a concurrent reader within organized screening programs.
