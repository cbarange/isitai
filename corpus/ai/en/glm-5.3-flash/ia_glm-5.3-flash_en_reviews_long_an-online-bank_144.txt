**A year with this online bank — honest thoughts from someone who was skeptical at first**

I'll start by admitting something: I was one of those people who swore I'd never trust an online-only bank. I'd been with a traditional high-street bank for over fifteen years, and the idea of not being able to walk into a branch and talk to a human being felt, frankly, reckless. My partner opened an account with this bank about a year before I did, and after months of watching her manage everything from her phone without a single complaint, I finally caved and opened my own account last spring. I wanted to wait a decent amount of time before writing this review, because I think reviews written after two weeks are mostly just first impressions. So here I am, a full year in, with a card, a savings account, and one international transfer behind me. Here's my honest take.

**The signup process**

Opening the account took me about twelve minutes, and I timed it because I was expecting it to be a nightmare. You scan your ID, take a short video selfie, answer a few questions about your income and where your money comes from, and that's basically it. My account was verified and usable the same evening. Compare that to my old bank, where opening my original account involved booking an appointment, taking a morning off work, and signing what felt like a ream of paper. This part genuinely exceeded my expectations.

**Everyday banking**

The app is where this bank lives or dies, and I'm happy to say it's very good. It's fast, clean, and doesn't crash on me. My favourite feature, and the one I didn't know I needed, is the automatic categorisation of spending. I can see exactly how much I've spent on groceries, transport, subscriptions, and eating out each month, laid out in a simple chart. It's honestly made me more aware of my habits than any budgeting spreadsheet I ever half-heartedly maintained. Instant notifications on every payment also mean I'd catch any fraudulent transaction within seconds.

Free card payments abroad at the real exchange rate was the other big selling point for me. I took a trip to Portugal in September and used the card everywhere — restaurants, a train ticket, a small surf shop in a village — and the amounts deducted matched exactly what I expected. No sneaky fees, no terrible conversion rate. My old bank would have charged me something for every single one of those transactions.

**Customer service — the real test**

This is what I was most worried about, and I want to be fair here: it's mostly been good, with one frustrating exception. When I had a question about setting up my savings account, I used the in-app chat and got a real, helpful answer within a few minutes. When my card got swallowed by a poorly designed ATM in Lisbon (not the bank's fault), I called the support line, got through in under five minutes, and the card was blocked and a replacement ordered before I'd even finished my coffee.

The one bad experience: a transfer I sent to a friend in another EU country took four days instead of the one or two advertised. I chased it twice via chat and got polite but vague answers both times. It eventually arrived, no money was lost, but the lack of a clear explanation annoyed me. To their credit, when I mentioned it later in a satisfaction survey, someone actually followed up with an apology and an explanation about a verification check. So mixed, but leaning positive.

**Things worth knowing before you sign up**

Being honest means mentioning the downsides, so here they are. First, no physical branches means no cash deposits if that's something you need — you can withdraw cash for free within generous limits, but depositing it is a different story. If you deal in cash regularly, this probably isn't the bank for you. Second, the joint account feature works fine, but setting it up required my partner to jump through a few more verification hoops than I expected. Third, the savings interest rate is decent but not spectacular; I keep my emergency fund there for convenience, but I compare rates elsewhere for anything bigger.

**Would I recommend it?**

Yes, with the caveats above. For someone like me — salaried, mostly card-based, travels a couple of times a year, hates queuing at branches — it has been genuinely excellent. The app, the free foreign payments, and the responsive support have converted a lifelong skeptic. My partner and I have since moved our shared expenses over to a joint account here, and my old bank is now down to a single dormant account I keep out of pure sentimental stubbornness.

Four stars, not five, because of that transfer hiccup and because I'd like to see slightly better savings rates. But I'd sign up again tomorrow without hesitation, and I've already recommended it to two friends who opened accounts after I wouldn't stop talking about it. That's probably the most sincere endorsement I can give.
