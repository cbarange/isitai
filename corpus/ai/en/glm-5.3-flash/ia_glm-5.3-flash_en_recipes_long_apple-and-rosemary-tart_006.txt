Apple and Rosemary Tart

This apple and rosemary tart is a wonderful departure from the usual sweet apple desserts. The rosemary adds an unexpected savory, pine-like note that pairs beautifully with the sweetness of the apples and the buttery crust. A touch of honey and a sprinkle of coarse sugar finish it off with just enough sweetness to make it feel like dessert, though it works equally well as an afternoon treat with tea or coffee.
Ingredients

For the crust:
1 ½ cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
½ teaspoon salt
1 tablespoon granulated sugar
½ cup (115 g) unsalted butter, cold and cut into small cubes
1 egg yolk
3 to 4 tablespoons ice water

For the filling:
5 to 6 medium apples (a mix of tart and sweet varieties works best, such as Granny Smith and Honeycrisp)
2 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
3 tablespoons honey or brown sugar
2 teaspoons fresh rosemary, finely chopped (plus a few whole small sprigs for decoration)
1 teaspoon vanilla extract
Juice of half a lemon
1 tablespoon coarse sugar for sprinkling
1 egg, beaten (for egg wash)
Instructions

Step 1: Make the dough.
In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, salt, and sugar. Add the cold butter cubes and work them into the flour with your fingertips or a pastry cutter until the mixture resembles coarse crumbs with some pea-sized pieces of butter remaining. In a small bowl, whisk the egg yolk with 3 tablespoons of ice water, then drizzle it over the flour mixture. Stir gently with a fork until the dough just comes together, adding the remaining tablespoon of water if it seems too dry. Gather the dough into a ball, flatten it into a disk, wrap it in plastic wrap, and refrigerate for at least 1 hour.

Step 2: Prepare the apples.
While the dough chills, peel, core, and slice the apples into thin wedges, about ¼ inch thick. Place them in a large bowl and toss with the lemon juice to prevent browning. Add the melted butter, honey, chopped rosemary, and vanilla extract, and toss everything together until the apple slices are evenly coated. Let the mixture sit for 15 to 20 minutes so the flavors meld and the apples release a little of their juice.

Step 3: Roll out the dough.
Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C) and line a large baking sheet with parchment paper. On a lightly floured surface, roll the chilled dough into a rough circle about 12 inches in diameter and ⅛ inch thick. It does not need to be perfectly round; this is a rustic tart, and slightly uneven edges add to its charm. Carefully transfer the dough to the prepared baking sheet.

Step 4: Assemble the tart.
Leaving a 2-inch border around the edge, arrange the apple slices in overlapping concentric circles, starting from the outside and working your way in. You can lay them flat or shingle them slightly upright for a more dramatic look. Tuck a few small whole rosemary sprigs between the slices here and there. Pour any remaining juices from the bowl over the arranged apples. Fold the border of dough up and over the outer edge of the apples, pleating it every couple of inches as you go. The center of the tart should remain open, showing off the apple pattern.

Step 5: Add the finishing touches.
Brush the folded crust generously with the beaten egg wash, which will give it a deep golden shine when baked. Sprinkle the crust and the exposed apples with the coarse sugar. The sugar will caramelize slightly in the oven, creating little pockets of crunch.

Step 6: Bake.
Bake the tart in the preheated oven for 40 to 45 minutes, rotating the baking sheet halfway through, until the crust is deep golden brown and the apples are tender and caramelized at the edges. If the crust browns too quickly, tent it loosely with foil for the last 10 to 15 minutes of baking.

Step 7: Rest and serve.
Remove the tart from the oven and let it cool on the baking sheet for at least 15 minutes. This resting time allows the juices released during baking to settle and thicken slightly. Just before serving, you can drizzle a little extra honey over the apples and scatter a few more rosemary leaves on top for fragrance.

Slice the tart into wedges and serve warm or at room temperature. It is delicious on its own, but a spoonful of crème fraîche, a dollop of lightly whipped cream, or a small scoop of vanilla ice cream takes it to another level. Leftovers keep well, loosely covered, at room temperature for one day or refrigerated for up to three days; reheat briefly in a warm oven to restore the crispness of the crust.

Tips:
Toast the chopped rosemary in the melted butter for 30 seconds before tossing with the apples to deepen its flavor.
If you prefer a softer rosemary presence, steep the sprig in warm honey for 10 minutes, then remove it and use the infused honey in the filling.
