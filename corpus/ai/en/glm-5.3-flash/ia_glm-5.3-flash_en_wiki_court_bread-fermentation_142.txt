Bread fermentation is the biological process in which yeasts and bacteria, primarily Saccharomyces cerevisiae and lactic acid bacteria, metabolize sugars in dough, producing carbon dioxide and ethanol that leaven and flavor bread. Fermentation duration, temperature, and hydration significantly influence crumb structure, taste, and digestibility. Traditional sourdough relies on wild cultures, while commercial baker's yeast accelerates industrial production. Studies suggest longer fermentation may reduce phytic acid and improve nutrient bioavailability.
