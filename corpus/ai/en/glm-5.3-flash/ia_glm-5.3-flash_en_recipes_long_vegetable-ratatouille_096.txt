**Ingredients (serves 4–6):**

- 2 medium eggplants (about 1 pound total)
- 3 medium zucchini
- 2 red bell peppers
- 2 yellow bell peppers
- 4 ripe tomatoes (or 1 can, 14 oz, of good-quality whole peeled tomatoes)
- 2 large yellow onions
- 4 cloves of garlic
- 5 tablespoons olive oil, divided
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1 teaspoon dried thyme)
- 1 sprig fresh rosemary
- 2 bay leaves
- 1/4 cup fresh basil leaves, torn, plus extra for garnish
- 1 tablespoon tomato paste
- 1/2 cup dry white wine (optional, may substitute vegetable broth)
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper, to taste
- A pinch of sugar (optional, to balance acidity)
- 1/4 cup chopped fresh parsley (optional)

**Equipment:**

A large, heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven with a lid, a large skillet, a sharp knife, a wooden spoon, and a cutting board.

**Step 1: Prepare the vegetables.**

Wash and dry all the vegetables thoroughly. Cut the eggplants into 1-inch cubes, leaving the skin on for color and texture. Sprinkle the cubes with about a teaspoon of salt, place them in a colander, and let them sit for 20–30 minutes over the sink. This draws out excess moisture and any bitterness. Meanwhile, cut the zucchini into half-moons about 3/4 inch thick, core and seed the bell peppers, and cut them into 1-inch pieces. Peel the onions and slice them thinly. Mince the garlic finely. If using fresh tomatoes, score an X on the bottom of each, blanch them in boiling water for 30 seconds, transfer to ice water, peel off the skins, remove the seeds, and chop the flesh coarsely.

**Step 2: Cook the eggplant.**

Rinse the salted eggplant under cold water and pat completely dry with paper towels. Heat 2 tablespoons of olive oil in a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the eggplant in a single layer — work in batches if necessary to avoid overcrowding, which causes steaming instead of browning. Cook for 6–8 minutes, turning occasionally, until the cubes are golden and tender. Transfer to a plate and set aside.

**Step 3: Sear the zucchini and peppers.**

In the same skillet, add 1 more tablespoon of olive oil. Add the zucchini and cook for 4–5 minutes until lightly golden at the edges but still slightly firm. Remove and set aside with the eggplant. Add the final 2 tablespoons of oil if the pan looks dry, then add the bell peppers. Cook for 5–6 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they soften and develop a few charred spots. Set aside with the other vegetables.

**Step 4: Build the flavor base.**

In a large Dutch oven or heavy pot, heat a small drizzle of olive oil over medium heat. Add the sliced onions with a pinch of salt and cook for 8–10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they are soft, translucent, and beginning to caramelize. Add the minced garlic and cook for 1 minute more, just until fragrant — do not let it brown. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 1–2 minutes, stirring constantly, to deepen its flavor.

**Step 5: Add the tomatoes and aromatics.**

Add the chopped fresh tomatoes (or canned tomatoes, crushed by hand) to the pot. Pour in the white wine if using, and let it bubble for 2 minutes to cook off the alcohol. Add the thyme, rosemary, and bay leaves. Season with salt, pepper, and a pinch of sugar if the tomatoes taste sharp. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer, then reduce the heat to low, cover partially, and let it cook for 15 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens slightly and the tomatoes break down.

**Step 6: Combine everything.**

Return the cooked eggplant, zucchini, and peppers to the pot. Stir gently to coat all the vegetables in the tomato base — take care not to break them apart; the beauty of ratatouille lies in keeping the pieces distinct. Cover the pot partially and simmer over low heat for 25–30 minutes, stirring carefully every 8–10 minutes. The vegetables should become tender and meld together while still holding their shape. If the mixture seems dry, add a splash of water or broth; if too watery, simmer uncovered for the last 10 minutes.

**Step 7: Finish and rest.**

Remove the pot from the heat. Fish out the bay leaves and woody herb stems. Stir in the torn basil and chopped parsley. Taste and adjust the seasoning — ratatouille often needs one more pinch of salt and a final grind of pepper. For the best flavor, let the dish rest for at least 15 minutes before serving; it is actually even better the next day, once the flavors have had time to deepen and marry overnight in the refrigerator.

**Serving suggestions:**

Serve ratatouille warm, at room temperature, or even chilled. It pairs beautifully with crusty bread, grilled meats, roast chicken, or simply a fried egg on top. A drizzle of your best extra-virgin olive oil and a few fresh basil leaves just before serving elevate the dish. Leftovers keep for 4–5 days refrigerated and can be stirred into pasta, folded into omelets, or spooned over grains like couscous or farro.
