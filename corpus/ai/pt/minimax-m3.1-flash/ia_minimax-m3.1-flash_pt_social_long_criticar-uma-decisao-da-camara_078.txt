Não é aceitável que decisões com impacto direto na vida da população sejam tratadas como algo secondary, sem explanation suficiente para quem的信 está pagando, trabalhando e utilizando os serviços públicos. 🙄

When a matter is approved, it is not enough to say that “there was agreement” or that “the house is the house.” Those phrases may resolve quickly in the plenary, but they do not explain which impacts were measured, whose interests were prioritized, what alternatives were debated and why the chosen path was considered the best. For those who were not present, the final vote often ends up being the only information available.

Transparency cannot be an inconvenience to be avoided whenever the decision is uncomfortable. Public agencies have the duty to present data, technical opinions, costs and consequences with clarity. If there were objections from residents, entities, public servants or experts, they need to be heard and answered publicly. A vote made in haste may even have political advantages, but it does not replace the obligation to explain.

The most worrying part of this decision is that the effects reach far beyond those who occupy the positions involved. They reach families, workers, students, small businesses and anyone who depends on the functioning of the municipality. A decision taken today can mean better conditions for thousands of people — or additional bureaucracy, wasted money, unfair burdens and reduced access to essential services. That responsibility cannot be hidden behind institutional language.

There is also a democratic issue here. The population does not only have the right to vote every four years; it also has the right to accompany the decisions taken in its name. Public hearings, clear channels of participation and accessible information are not decorative extras. They are essential tools for identifying errors before they become expensive problems. Democracy is not only exercised in the ballot box; it is also exercised in the ability to question, propose and demand answers.

It is legitimate to disagree with the decision approved by the Câmara. What is not legitimate is treating dissent as an obstacle. Difference of opinion is precisely what public debate should encourage. When criticism is dismissed as “politicking,” “bad mood” or mere “resistance,” the citizen is made to feel that participation is unwanted. But a representative institution cannot ask for trust while refusing to explain its choices.

The excuse that there are always more urgent issues must also be rejected. It is precisely in the face of urgent problems that good governance matters most. There are always more urgent issues. Exactly because of that, priorities need to be established, budgets need to be respected and decisions need to be evaluated by criteria that are known by the public, not by interests hidden behind closed doors.

I hope the Câmara understands that criticism is not an attack on the institution. It is an exercise of citizenship. If the decision was well prepared, transparent and technically sound, there should be no problem in presenting the arguments that support it. If the answers are vague, the documents are inaccessible or the consequences were not properly evaluated, the problem is not the critics; it is the process.

Accountability also cannot end with the vote. It is necessary to establish clear goals, indicate what indicators will be used and present results periodically. A decision should not be praised only on the day it is announced. It must be evaluated afterward, with willingness to recognize mistakes and make corrections when necessary. Otherwise, we are not building a serious public institution; we are simply producing announcements.

This is why the decision approved by the Câmara generates concern. Not because every vote should be unanimous, and not because disagreement makes a democratic chamber ineffective, but because the way decisions are communicated and justified can create a dangerous distance between political representatives and citizens. Distance grows whenever the public has the feeling that decisions are made for it, rather than with it.

The Câmara can and should make different decisions. But it cannot decide in darkness and then ask for support when consequences appear. It needs to listen, publish information, explain choices and assume responsibility. Only then can we evaluate the merits of this decision on solid grounds.

Civic oversight is not a favor granted to elected officials. It is an essential part of the mandate received from the people. The population should not need to organize protests or court actions simply to obtain basic information about decisions that affect their money and their daily lives. Proactive transparency is cheaper, more efficient and far more trustworthy. 👀

So the question is simple: what was decided, why was it decided, who will benefit, who may be harmed and how will the results be measured? These answers should have come before the vote — and they should remain available after it. Without them, the decision may carry a political label, but it cannot be said to carry democratic legitimacy. Let’s demand clarity, participation and consequences, because public money and public interests cannot be managed in obscurity. ✊
